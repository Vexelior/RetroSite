The 216 Web-Safe Colors

In the mid-90s, most home computers had 8-bit video cards, which could only show 256 colors at once. Windows and the Macintosh each kept some of those 256 slots for themselves, and the two systems didn't keep the same ones. If you picked a color outside the shared set, the browser would dither it into a speckled pattern of nearby colors, and your nice smooth logo turned grainy.

The fix was a palette of colors that would look the same on both platforms. Each of red, green, and blue could only be one of six values: 00, 33, 66, 99, CC, or FF. That's 6 x 6 x 6 = 216 colors. Then vs. now: by the early 2000s most computers showed thousands or millions of colors, and the web-safe palette wasn't needed anymore. Today, CSS can describe colors in wide-gamut color spaces that even the best 90s monitors couldn't show, and designers pick colors for contrast and accessibility instead of for the video card.
